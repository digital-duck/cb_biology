Carbon Dating

Carbon dating is a radiometric method for estimating the age of organic material by measuring the residual proportion of carbon-14 ($^{14}\mathrm{C}$), a radioactive isotope of carbon that decays via beta emission with a well-characterized half-life. While an organism is alive, it continuously exchanges carbon with its environment, maintaining a roughly constant ratio of $^{14}\mathrm{C}$ to stable $^{12}\mathrm{C}$. Once the organism dies, this exchange ceases, and the $^{14}\mathrm{C}$ present begins to decay according to the exponential law

$$N(t) = N_0 e^{-\lambda t},$$

where $N_0$ is the initial quantity of $^{14}\mathrm{C}$, $\lambda$ is the decay constant, and $t$ is elapsed time since death.

Worked example. The half-life of $^{14}\mathrm{C}$ is $t_{1/2} = 5730$ years, giving $\lambda = \ln 2 / t_{1/2} \approx 1.21 \times 10^{-4}\ \text{yr}^{-1}$. Suppose a fossil sample retains 35% of its original $^{14}\mathrm{C}$ activity. Solving $0.35 = e^{-\lambda t}$ yields

$$t = \frac{-\ln(0.35)}{\lambda} \approx \frac{1.0498}{1.21 \times 10^{-4}} \approx 8676 \text{ years}.$$

Key theorem. Uniqueness of the decay-based age estimate. Since $N(t) = N_0 e^{-\lambda t}$ is strictly monotonic decreasing for $\lambda > 0$, the map from the fraction remaining $N(t)/N_0 \in (0,1]$ to elapsed time $t$ is a bijection. Consequently, given an accurate measurement of the remaining fraction and known $\lambda$, the elapsed time $t$ is uniquely determined — the mathematical foundation guaranteeing that carbon dating yields a single, well-defined age rather than multiple candidate solutions.

Lab cell (SymPy).

import sympy as sp

t, lam, N0, N = sp.symbols('t lambda N0 N', positive=True)
half_life = 5730
lam_val = sp.log(2) / half_life

fraction_remaining = sp.Rational(35, 100)
age = sp.solve(sp.Eq(fraction_remaining, sp.exp(-lam * t)), t)[0]
age_years = age.subs(lam, lam_val)
print(sp.N(age_years, 6))  # ~8676.29 years

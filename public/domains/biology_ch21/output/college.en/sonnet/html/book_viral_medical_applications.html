Every concept in this book—modeling, data structures, statistical inference, algorithmic reasoning—converges here, in the domain where getting the answer right can mean the difference between a correct diagnosis and a missed one. Viral medical applications is the natural endpoint of this book not because viruses are the most dramatic subject, but because they are the subject that most ruthlessly demands everything you have learned to be used together, correctly, and at scale. A virus is a small, fast-mutating, exponentially spreading object, and understanding it requires you to move fluidly between sequence data, population dynamics, probability, and computation. There is no single "viral medicine" formula to memorize; there is a way of thinking that this book has been building toward all along, and this is where it finally gets applied to something that matters immediately and personally.

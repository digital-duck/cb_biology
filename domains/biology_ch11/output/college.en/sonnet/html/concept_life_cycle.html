Life Cycle

A life cycle is the complete sequence of developmental stages an organism passes through from one generation to the next, defined by the alternation between cells carrying a single set of chromosomes (haploid, $n$) and cells carrying two sets (diploid, $2n$). Two processes drive the transitions between these states: meiosis, which halves chromosome number ($2n \rightarrow n$), and fertilization, which restores it ($n + n \rightarrow 2n$). Every sexually reproducing organism's life cycle can be described by where meiosis falls relative to fertilization, and how much of the cycle each ploidy state occupies.

The one new idea to master here is alternation of generations: a life cycle in which a multicellular diploid stage (the sporophyte) and a multicellular haploid stage (the gametophyte) both exist, each produced from the other. Plants and some algae follow this pattern. The sporophyte undergoes meiosis to produce haploid spores; each spore divides by ordinary mitosis into a multicellular gametophyte. The gametophyte then produces gametes by mitosis — not meiosis — and two gametes fuse to restore the diploid sporophyte.

Worked example. Compare alternation of generations to the simpler pattern in humans. In humans, the body is diploid throughout, and meiosis directly produces haploid gametes with no multicellular haploid stage in between. In a plant like moss, meiosis instead produces a spore, and that spore grows into an entire haploid organism (the gametophyte) before gametes are ever made. The distinguishing question is: does meiosis produce gametes directly, or does it produce spores that first grow into a multicellular haploid body? Answering this correctly identifies which pattern you're looking at.

Problem-solving application. A moss plant ($n = 7$) produces spores by meiosis. The resulting gametophyte grows for several weeks before producing gametes. Predict the chromosome number of (a) the gametophyte's body cells, (b) its gametes, and (c) the zygote. Since the gametophyte arises from a meiotic spore without any further change in ploidy, its body cells are haploid: $n = 7$. Its gametes, produced by ordinary mitosis, remain haploid: $n = 7$. Fertilization then combines two gametes into a diploid zygote: $2n = 14$. Tracking each meiosis and fertilization event, rather than memorizing labels, is what lets you solve life-cycle problems for any unfamiliar organism.

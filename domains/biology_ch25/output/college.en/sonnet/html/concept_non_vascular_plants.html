Non Vascular Plants

Non-vascular plants — the bryophytes, comprising liverworts, hornworts, and mosses — are the simplest living land plants, defined by the absence of vascular tissue: the internal pipeline system that moves water and nutrients through most other plants. Without it, individuals stay small, typically a few centimeters tall, because water and dissolved sugars must pass cell-to-cell or by diffusion rather than through a pressurized transport network. Their reproductive cycle also looks different from ferns or flowering plants: most of a bryophyte's life is spent as the visible green, leafy or flat structure, while a second, dependent generation grows briefly out of it to produce spores and then withers. Fertilization itself requires a film of water, since the sperm must physically swim from one reproductive structure to the egg in another.

Worked example. Consider why mosses are almost always found in damp, shaded habitats — stream banks, forest floors, north-facing rock faces — rather than open, dry fields. Two consequences of lacking vascular tissue explain this. First, mosses cannot draw water up from soil or resist rapid drying, so they absorb water directly across their surface and thrive only where moisture is locally available. Second, because fertilization depends on sperm swimming through surface water to reach an egg, reproduction fails outright in dry conditions. A moss growing on a sunny rock in a drought can survive by drying out and going dormant, but it cannot reproduce sexually until rain or dew supplies enough water for sperm to travel.

Problem-solving application. Suppose you are a field ecologist and want to use moss presence as a rapid, low-cost indicator of local moisture. Given the biology above, predict and justify: (1) which of two adjacent forest plots — one with a closed, humid canopy and one recently logged and sun-exposed — would show higher moss diversity, and (2) whether finding abundant moss spore-capsules (the short-lived dependent stage, not just the green mats) is a good sign of a recent rain event. For (1), reason from water dependence for both growth and fertilization. For (2), reason from the fact that standing water is needed specifically at the moment of fertilization, several weeks before the spore-capsules mature and become visible.
